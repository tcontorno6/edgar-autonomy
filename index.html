What six companies' SEC filings actually say about robotaxis

Robotaxis are among the most hyped stories in tech and autos. This measures what Tesla, Alphabet, GM, Amazon, Uber and Lyft put in the one place that carries legal liability: their filings. Every number below traces to the exact sentence it came from.

FINDING 01

GM talks about robotaxis the most. The companies actually running them barely appear.

Robotaxi-service mentions per 1,000 words of filing text, pooled across 2019–2026. Waymo and Zoox, the two services carrying real driverless passengers, are nearly invisible in their parents' filings.

Two dynamics over time

GM's attention swells through the Cruise era; Tesla's is near zero until its 2025 Robotaxi launch. Uber, Lyft, Waymo and Zoox stay near the floor throughout.

FINDING 02

Across 1,137 documents, the six disclosed 29 operational numbers.

Distinct operational autonomy numbers stated in filing text — miles, rides, cities, fleet, safety. Twenty-seven are the companies' own; two are U.S.-average crash benchmarks Tesla quotes. The only driverless operating figure in seven years is Waymo's weekly ride count.

FINDING 03

Talk and action run in reverse.

Company-reported numbers per 100 counted autonomy mentions. The companies that talk most — Uber, Lyft, GM, largely inside risk factors — back it with the fewest numbers. The operators that mention it least do so almost only to report something.

FINDING 04

The "everyone's negative on autonomy" read is a risk-factor mirage.

Forty percent of autonomy passages sit in risk factors, which are negative by design. Read raw, every company looks sour on autonomy. Controlled against the non-autonomy text of the same sections, it flips.

Before the control: net tone looks negative for all — Lyft −20.8, Uber −7.7, GM −6.6, Tesla −5.9. After it: five of six write about autonomy measurably more positively than the surrounding text. Only Lyft is indistinguishable from its baseline.

FINDING 05

GM's robotaxi language is the sound of a wind-down — and it turns in late 2023.

GM's heavy attention in Finding 01 is not enthusiasm. In the robotaxi passages outside boilerplate, tone falls off a cliff at 2023Q4 — a year before GM publicly pulled funding from Cruise's robotaxi work in December 2024.

Cruise / robotaxi tone vs. baseline, by quarter

Above zero = more positive than the surrounding text; below = more negative. The negativity is financial — restructuring, losses, impairment — not regulatory.

What the language is doing: wind-down vs. expansion vocabulary

Share of GM robotaxi passages using wind-down words (pause, restructure, no longer fund…) vs. expansion words (launch, scale, new markets…).

2025+ vs. 2019–2024 (permutation test, 20,000 shuffles): net tone −0.8 → −14.7 (p<0.001); wind-down vocabulary 7% → 66% (p<0.001). The turn begins at the October 2023 pedestrian accident, not the December 2024 funding decision.
METHOD & LIMITS

How it was built, and where it is weak.

Four phases, each validated on a held-out sample with the rules frozen before drawing it. All analysis is local and free; no paid model was used at any step.

Pipeline

  • Corpus. EDGAR 10-K / 10-Q / 8-K (with EX-99 exhibits); CIKs verified; 0 fetch or clean errors.
  • Attention. Curated keyword set, two groups, word-boundary and case-sensitive matching; table and corporate-transaction hits excluded.
  • Quantification. Regex + a local relevance model; every number must match its source sentence at stored offsets. 0 citation failures.
  • Stance. Loughran-McDonald lexicons, scored on the autonomy sentence only, reported relative to same-section baseline.

Validation (held-out)

Read with these in mind

  • Lexicon positive class. Loughran-McDonald counts gain and benefit regardless of subject; short accounting sentences can score as upbeat. Positive-tone levels should be read with care (10/15 positive extremes matched a human).
  • Text only. Earnings-call transcripts, where most operational numbers are spoken, are out of scope. This measures what reaches the legal record.
  • Chart-only figures excluded. Tesla's paid Robotaxi miles appear only as chart axes; the extractor rejects them rather than invent a value.
  • Small samples. Alphabet (1 number) and Amazon (4) are descriptive, not trends. One reviewer set all labels; there is no inter-rater measure.
